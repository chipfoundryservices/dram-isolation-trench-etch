# Glossary: DRAM Isolation Trench Etch

Terms are defined as they are used in this book. Chapter references point to the main discussion.

---

## A

**AA (active area):** Silicon island that holds the access transistors of two DRAM cells, surrounded by isolation trench. Reference: 14 nm wide, 84 nm long, 32 nm pitch. (Ch. 1.2)

**AA bridge:** Unetched or collapsed silicon joining two neighboring AAs, so their storage nodes share a body. Caused by particles, collapse, or missing spaces. (Ch. 9.5, Ch. 11.2.4)

**Actinometry:** Ratio of an emission line to a nearby Ar line used to track relative species density (e.g., O 777 / Ar 750). (App. B.5.1)

**APC (advanced process control):** Feed-forward and feedback adjustment of etch time and secondary knobs from incoming and post-etch measurements. (Ch. 15.4)

**ARDE (aspect-ratio-dependent etching):** Decrease of etch rate with aspect ratio, which makes narrow cell trenches shallower than periphery trenches. Described by ER/ER₀ = 1/(1 + kA). (Ch. 3.3–3.5)

**ARDE coefficient (k):** Parameter of the linear ARDE form. Reference 0.025 (pulsed); 0.058 uncompensated. k ≈ β/3. (Ch. 3.3.3)

**Array edge:** Boundary of the cell array where SAQP lines end; protected by dummy AA lines. (Ch. 2.6.2, Ch. 11.3.2)

**Aspect ratio (A):** Depth (plus mask height, for transport) divided by trench width. Reference 13.9 (Si) and 16.6 (with mask). (Ch. 1.5)

---

## B

**BC (storage-node contact, buried contact):** Contact landing on an island end, connecting the cell transistor to the capacitor. (Ch. 1.2.2, Ch. 16.6)

**BCAT (buried-channel array transistor):** DRAM access transistor whose word line is buried in a trench across the AA, with a channel wrapping beneath it. (Ch. 1.3)

**Bias:** RF power applied to the wafer chuck to accelerate ions; sets ion energy. (Ch. 5.3, Ch. 6)

**Bottom reaction probability (β):** Probability that a halogen atom reaching the trench bottom reacts. Sets ARDE in the Coburn–Winters model. (Ch. 3.3.2)

**Bow:** Local widening of the trench (narrowing of the AA) just below the mask, caused by ions reflected from mask facets. (Ch. 10.4.2)

**Breakthrough (BT):** Short step removing native oxide from exposed silicon before the main etch. Reference CF₄/He, 8 s. (Ch. 4.4)

---

## C

**Capillary collapse:** Pulling together of neighboring fins by the Laplace pressure of drying liquid. (Ch. 11.2)

**CD-SAXS:** Transmission small-angle X-ray scattering that measures the average profile of a periodic array. (Ch. 15.2)

**Cell pre-etch:** Dual-depth scheme in which the cell is etched alone first, with the periphery masked. (Ch. 14.1.2)

**Coburn–Winters model:** ER_bottom/ER_open = K/(K + β(1 − K)). (Ch. 3.3.2)

**Collapse margin (M):** Ratio of fin bending stiffness to destabilizing capillary stiffness; stable if M > 1. Reference 1.48 with IPA, 0.45 with water. (Ch. 11.2.2)

**Cut (AA cut):** Lithography and etch that divide AA lines into islands. (Ch. 2.4)

**Cut gap (G):** Space between the ends of two islands on the same line. Reference 24 nm. Etches deeper than line spaces. (Ch. 1.2.2, Ch. 12.4)

---

## D

**DC (bit-line contact, direct contact):** Contact at the center of an island, shared by its two cells. (Ch. 1.2.2)

**Depth series:** Set of wafers etched for different times to measure depth and profile versus time for each feature. (Ch. 7.7, App. C.1)

**Depth walk:** Periodic depth variation across the array caused by pitch walk through ARDE. ≈ 2.75 nm of depth per nm of space. (Ch. 12.3)

**D_it:** Interface trap density at the Si/SiO₂ liner interface, per cm² per eV. (Ch. 13.1.4)

**Dual depth:** Any scheme that gives the cell and periphery different etch times. (Ch. 14.1)

**Dummy lines:** Non-functional AA lines at the array edge that absorb asymmetric etch, drying, and fill loads. (Ch. 2.6.2, Ch. 11.3.2)

---

## E

**Edge ring:** Ring around the wafer on the chuck that shapes the sheath at the wafer edge; wears with RF hours. (Ch. 9.3)

**Edge tilt:** Inclination of trenches near the wafer edge caused by a bent sheath. θ_e ≈ Δ/(2s). (Ch. 9.3.1)

**ESC (electrostatic chuck):** Wafer holder providing clamping, bias, and multi-zone temperature control. (Ch. 8)

**EWMA:** Exponentially weighted moving average used in feedback control. (Ch. 15.4.2)

---

## F

**Facet:** Inclined surface formed at mask-line corners by angle-dependent sputtering. (Ch. 4.3.2)

**FDC (fault detection and classification):** Monitoring of tool signals per step against control limits. (Ch. 15.3)

**Fill limit:** Minimum trench bottom width (≈ 7 nm) for void-free fill after the liner. (Ch. 10.2.2)

**Fin:** The silicon wall left between two trenches; the AA in cross-section. (Ch. 11.1)

---

## G

**GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage):** Band-to-band tunneling leakage at the junction under a word-line edge. (Ch. 13.1.1)

**Grass (silicon grass):** Silicon spikes left in wide trenches by micromasking. (Ch. 9.5.2)

---

## H

**HBr/Cl₂/O₂ chemistry:** The standard silicon trench chemistry; Br for selectivity and self-passivation, Cl for rate, O for passivation. (Ch. 4.1)

**h_m (mask height):** Total hard-mask thickness at the start of the silicon etch. Reference 49 nm. (Ch. 2.5)

---

## I

**ICP (inductively coupled plasma):** Reactor with a coil-driven source and separate wafer bias; the standard silicon-etch chamber. Also called TCP. (Ch. 5)

**IED (ion energy distribution):** Distribution of ion energies at the wafer, shaped by bias frequency. (Ch. 6.2)

**Island-end pull-back:** Lateral retreat of an island end during etch, 2–4 nm per end. (Ch. 12.4.2)

**ISSG (in-situ steam generation):** Radical oxidation process used for the liner; grows uniformly and rounds corners. (Ch. 13.3)

---

## L

**Laplace pressure:** Pressure difference across a curved meniscus; 2γ cos θ / s in a slot. (Ch. 11.2.1)

**Line space (S):** Trench between two neighboring AA lines. Reference 18 nm at the top. (Ch. 1.2.2)

**Liner:** Thin thermal (radical) oxide grown on trench walls after the etch. Reference 2.5 nm, consuming 1.1 nm Si per side. (Ch. 13.3, Ch. 16.2)

**LER / LWR:** Line-edge roughness / line-width roughness. (Ch. 2.3)

---

## M

**Macroloading:** Dependence of the open-area etch rate on the total exposed silicon fraction. (Ch. 3.5.4)

**ME-1 / ME-2:** First and second main-etch steps of the reference recipe; boundary near 120 nm depth. (Ch. 4.7, Ch. 7.3)

**Micromasking:** Local blocking of the etch by small oxide nuclei or particles. (Ch. 9.5.2)

**Microtrench:** Groove at the bottom corners of a trench from ions reflected off the lower sidewalls. (Ch. 10.3.2)

---

## O

**OCD (optical critical dimension):** Model-based scatterometry for depth, CD, and profile. (Ch. 15.2)

**OES (optical emission spectroscopy):** Monitoring of plasma emission lines for endpoint and faults. (Ch. 15.1)

---

## P

**Passing word line:** Word line that runs through isolation beside an island it does not control. (Ch. 1.2.2, Ch. 1.4.3)

**Passivation (SiOₓBrᵧ):** Silicon oxybromide film formed on trench walls from etch products and oxygen; makes the etch anisotropic. (Ch. 4.2)

**Periphery:** Non-array circuitry (sense amplifiers, drivers, decoders, I/O) with wider and deeper isolation. (Ch. 2.6, Ch. 12)

**Pitch walk:** Systematic difference between the α, β, and γ spaces of SAQP. (Ch. 2.2)

**Profile step (PS):** Final low-energy step that rounds trench bottoms. (Ch. 7.3.3, Ch. 10.3.3)

**Pulsing (synchronized):** On/off modulation of source and bias RF in phase. Raises neutral-to-ion ratio and lowers k. (Ch. 6.3)

---

## R

**Retention tail:** The small fraction of cells with abnormally high leakage that sets the refresh interval. (Ch. 1.1.2, Ch. 13)

**Row hammer:** Disturbance of a cell's stored charge by repeated activation of a nearby (often passing) word line. (Ch. 1.4.3)

---

## S

**Saddle fin:** Short silicon fin under a buried word line where the isolation oxide is recessed deeper than the silicon. Reference height 40 nm. (Ch. 1.3.2)

**SAQP (self-aligned quadruple patterning):** Two-spacer process making 32 nm-pitch lines from 128 nm-pitch lithography. (Ch. 2.1)

**Season:** Plasma deposition of a reproducible SiO₂-like coating on chamber walls after each clean. (Ch. 9.2)

**Self-limited depth:** Depth at which a tapered narrow trench closes and stops etching, independent of time. (Ch. 10.2.4)

**Sidewall angle (α, SWA):** Deviation of the trench wall from vertical. Reference 1.0°; window 0.7–1.25°. (Ch. 10.2)

**SRH generation:** Shockley–Read–Hall carrier generation via deep-level traps; source of junction leakage. (Ch. 13.1.2)

**SN (storage node):** The capacitor electrode and its junction in the island end. (Ch. 1.1)

**Spacer-is-line / spacer-is-space:** SAQP tone choices; lines (AA) or spaces (trenches) are the spacer-defined features. (Ch. 2.2.4)

---

## T

**TAT (trap-assisted tunneling):** Field-enhanced leakage through deep traps in high-field regions. (Ch. 13.1.3)

**Taper:** Positive sidewall angle; narrows the trench with depth. (Ch. 10.2)

---

## V

**Virtual metrology:** Prediction of wafer results from tool and OES data. (Ch. 15.4.5)

**VCT (vertical channel transistor):** Access transistor in 4F² DRAM with a vertical channel and buried bit line. (Ch. 14.4)

---

## W

**W(z):** AA width at depth z. Reference W(140) = 18.9 nm. (Ch. 1.4.2, Ch. 10.5)

**WAC (waferless autoclean):** Plasma clean of chamber walls between wafers (NF₃/O₂). (Ch. 9.2)

**w_eff:** Equivalent uniform width of a tapered fin for bending. Reference 20.8 nm. (Ch. 11.1.2)

**Window (process):** Range of etch time in which the cell and all periphery depths are in specification. Reference 58.1–66.0 s. (Ch. 12.2)

**Word line (WL):** Gate line of the cell transistors; buried in the 6F² BCAT. (Ch. 1.3)
